## ED11. Monte Carlo inputs against outputs

## ED12. What drives the joint uncertainty

![ED12](ED12_mc_importance.png)

**ED12.** Standardised regression coefficients (SRC) and partial rank correlation coefficients (PRCC) of the cumulative LoE AR with percentile-bootstrap 95% intervals, for the Reference, P5 Combined (Moderate) and TL, beside the Sobol indices of the Reference carbon. (a) Standardised regression coefficients (linear model of the cumulative LoE AR on the four inputs; R² in the legend) and (b) partial rank correlation coefficients, each with its percentile bootstrap 95% interval (1,000 resamples of the draws; mcstats.py). (c) Sobol first-order and total indices of the Reference carbon 2026–2050 (MonteCarlo.py; Saltelli 2010 / Jansen estimators), where the carbon input is the bootstrap selector itself (variance-based indices need no order). Carbon-factor uncertainty barely moves the Reference LoE AR (PRCC -0.04): the AR budget is grandfathered from each draw's own 2025 carbon factors, so carbon and budget move together; under TL it lowers the LoE AR (PRCC -0.60) because TL's carbon targets are absolute while the budget scales with the factors.

## ED13. When does the theoretical limit stay within AR?

![ED13](ED13_conditional_tl.png)

**ED13.** TL cumulative LoE AR against the population rank of each draw, coloured by the carbon-factor index, with LoE AR = 1; logistic regression of P(TL LoE AR <= 1) on the sampled inputs (odds ratios with 95% intervals). TL: hyper-optimistic theoretical limit; a thought experiment, not a pathway. TL stays within the AR budget in 4,996 of 5,000 draws (99.92%; Wilson 95% 99.79–99.97%). The 4 draws above it are the draws with the highest population ranks (percentile ranks 99.96, 99.88, 99.98, 100.00); TL LoE AR 1.001, 1.000, 1.009, 1.130. (a) Shading: the AR and AtP budget zones (the AtP line at the deterministic B_AtP / B_AR = 0.250; across draws 0.217–0.281). (b) Firth penalised-likelihood logistic regression (Heinze & Schemper 2002) with profile-likelihood 95% intervals; the maximum-likelihood estimate does not exist (separation) for the population models. Odds ratio per SD of population rank, alone: 0.00066 (2.9e-09–0.035). With 4 exceedances (1 per variable in the multivariable model, below the conventional 10) the multivariable odds ratios are indicative only; the model is deterministic, so the intervals describe the 5,000-draw design. (c) Share of draws within the AR budget by population-rank quintile, Wilson 95% intervals.
