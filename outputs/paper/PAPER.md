## Findings

1. New Zealand builds 89.6 million m² of new residential floor area in 2026–2050 in the Reference (Monte Carlo 90% interval 69.1–109.9); 45% of it serves population growth, the only demand no pathway targets. (Source: data/pathways/runs.csv, data/montecarlo/summary_REF.csv, data/baseline/bands_annual.csv.)
2. Its embodied carbon is 21,578 kt CO₂e within 2026–2050 (Monte Carlo 16,678–26,596; materials A1–A3 14,760 kt), with a further 8,208–8,969 kt committed after 2050, 45–49 years of the 2050 acquired-rights boundary. (Source: data/baseline/annual_by_stage.csv, data/aesa/results.json, data/pathways/montecarlo_summary.csv.)
3. This exceeds the sector's share of the 2 °C carrying capacity 4.85-fold by acquired rights (Monte Carlo 3.90–5.75) and 19.4-fold by ability to pay (15.5–23.1); the two principles are reported side by side, never averaged. (Source: data/pathways/runs.csv, data/pathways/montecarlo_summary.csv.)
4. Combining the four pathways (P5, Moderate, full roll-out by 2035) lowers carbon 2026–2050 by 29%: the cumulative LoE AR falls from 4.85 to 3.44 (Monte Carlo 2.36–4.43; P(LoE AR ≤ 1) = 0%), far above the acquired-rights budget. (Source: data/pathways/runs.csv, data/pathways/montecarlo_summary.csv.)
5. Even the hyper-optimistic theoretical limit (TL; a thought experiment, not a pathway) reaches only LoE AR 0.63 (Monte Carlo 0.42–0.82; P(LoE AR ≤ 1) = 100%) and LoE AtP 2.5 (P(LoE AtP ≤ 1) = 0%); reaching the AR budget means not building nan dwellings in 2027–2050, i.e. housing nan new residents in existing dwellings. (Source: data/pathways/runs.csv, data/pathways/montecarlo_summary.csv, data/pathways/theoretical_limit.json.)
6. Of the gap between P5 and TL, the largest contributions are TL1 no extra space (0.64 of LoE AR) and TL3 no vacancy (0.44); starting at full strength in 2027 adds 0.19. (Source: data/pathways/shapley_theoretical_limit.csv.)
7. No cut of 2027–2050 floor area or carbon per m² reaches the ability-to-pay budget: the observed 2026 cohort alone gives a cumulative LoE AtP of 1.28. (Source: data/pathways/solution_space.csv, data/pathways/runs.csv.)

## Figures

### Fig1. How the model works

![Fig1](Fig1_model_schematic.png)

**Fig1.** Model structure. A bottom-up chain converts population and household projections into dwellings required, floor area by reason for building (demand bands) and embodied carbon, which is compared with two allocations of a global carrying capacity (acquired rights, AR; ability to pay, AtP). Pathway levers act on the band or quantity they target; only population growth is untargeted by design.

### Fig2. Why floor area is built

![Fig2](Fig2_demand_bands.png)

**Fig2.** Floor area and carbon by reason for building. Bands sum to the in-scope total. Only population growth is untargeted; smaller households, extra space, vacancy, the 2026 overbuild, historical replacement and the recent replacement surge are the bands the pathways act on. Over 2026–2050 the untargeted band (population growth) is 45% of the 89.6 million m² built (a) and 45% of the carbon (b). History (a): interval means between censuses (vacancy on one definition, the pooled 2018/2023 empty share); the net replacement is census-measured (the census-interval rate of the dwelling-count identity × the previous year's stock) and the hatched band is the building that household formation, vacancy and census-measured replacement leave unexplained (it may be negative); the faint column after the latest census uses consent-derived households. Retirement villages (out of scope) are removed from every band in proportion. (b): maintenance (B2, B4) within 2026–2050 is its own layer.

### Fig3. Embodied carbon of the Reference

![Fig3](Fig3_reference_carbon.png)

**Fig3.** Embodied carbon of new residential construction in the Reference. Materials (A1–A3), construction (A4–A5) and soil carbon loss are counted in the construction year, maintenance (B2, B4) in the year it occurs within 2026–2050; later events and end of life are committed after 2050 (c, hatched). Of the 21,578 kt CO₂e emitted in 2026–2050, materials (A1–A3) are 14,760 kt, construction (A4–A5) 3,364 kt, maintenance 403 kt and soil carbon loss 3,050 kt (a); by material (b) each material includes its maintenance within 2026–2050. A further 8,208–8,969 kt is committed after 2050 (later B2/B4 and C1–C4 of the 2026–2050 cohorts; lower bound with the 2050 factors held, upper bound at 2025 factors), 45–49 years of the 2050 acquired-rights boundary (183 kt a year; right axis of c). The committed emissions are not part of the level of exceedance 2026–2050.

### Fig5. Emissions against the boundaries, and what closes the gap

**Fig5.** Absolute sustainability of the Reference, P5 Combined and the theoretical limit. TL: hyper-optimistic theoretical limit; a thought experiment, not a pathway. ### Fig6. Solution space: demand and intensity

![Fig6](Fig6_solution_space.png)

**Fig6.** Solution space (after Horup et al. 2025, Fig. 6). Markers sit at each run's effective reductions of 2027–2050 floor area and non-soil carbon per m²; L8, L9, TL8 and TL9 also lower soil carbon, which the plane does not show. Cutting 2027–2050 construction alone by 85.0% would bring the Reference to LoE AR = 1 (back-cast); P5 sits at 22% demand and 9% intensity reduction, TL at 84% and 49% (at these coordinates the plane reads LoE AR 0.73; the TL run, with its lower soil carbon, 0.63).
